An independent account · reviewed 2 November 2026

I bought a mouse, five Microsoft licences and a Wi-Fi extender. I expected to receive them. This was my first misunderstanding of the arrangement.

Flipkart provided delivery dates, then improved them by moving them into the future. At one point, the Microsoft shipment might have been lost or damaged. This was useful information: until then I had only known it was late.

Flipkart eventually reported delivering the three orders. Along the way, I acquired a collection of support emails, several unanswered questions and a promised call that, as I later wrote, did not arrive. The emails proved much easier to deliver than the mouse.

The board above shows dates in 2026. For Microsoft 365, the first date is when the order was placed; its original delivery date was 30 September. The other dates are described in the account below.

The airport scene and product images are illustrations, not photographs of the actual deliveries.

The orders took the scenic route.

Three deliveries, as reported in the emails.

The mouse

Logitech MX Vertical

Promised 15 October. Flipkart reported delivery on 21 October.

Six days late. I had ordered a mouse and, at no extra charge, received a lesson in patience.

The licences

Microsoft 365 Personal × 5

Ordered 25 September, due 30 September. On 14 October, Flipkart said the shipment might have been lost or damaged and a replacement would be sent. Delivery was reported on 25 October.

A shipment can be late, lost or damaged. The correspondence allowed me to consider all three possibilities.

The extender

TP-Link Wi-Fi range extender

Due 22 October, then moved to 25 October. Flipkart reported delivery on 29 October.

It extended the delivery schedule before it had the opportunity to extend the Wi-Fi.

A separate Acer laptop stand had not shipped when I wrote on 28 October. Its later outcome is not established in the emails reviewed.

Then came the emails.

Selected moments from the correspondence. The commentary is mine.

17 October 2026

The grievance

I wrote about the delayed orders, asked for the products and disputed records attributing delivery-date changes to me. I also asked for a person who could take responsibility.

I had been asking for the items to move. I had not realised the dates were the part that came with free relocation.

20 October 2026

A larger audience

I sent an escalation addressed to Kalyan Krishnamurthy and John Furner at Flipkart and Walmart addresses, asking about delivery records and priority support. The correspondence reviewed does not establish whether either executive personally read it.

An escalation is an email with more important people in the address line and the same missing items at home. I can confirm the second part was working.

23 October 2026

One customer, several departments

I described a call in which the representative could see the mouse order but said other teams would handle the others. The mouse had arrived by then; I was still asking for one accountable contact.

This struck me as an elegant system. To find out about several orders, I merely needed several departments. To find one person responsible for all of them, I would presumably need another department.

26 October 2026

The administrative miracle

“Your concern is now our top priority.”

An automated message said a specialist would get in touch within 24 hours. I later wrote that the call had not arrived.

A separate grievance reply said it could see no earlier contact with support, despite the support emails already in my inbox. I could forward the existing emails as proof that the existing emails exist. This would create another email whose existence would presumably need to be established. At that point, my inbox might qualify for its own customer-support ticket.

28–29 October 2026

Another escalation, then a delay notice

I wrote that the Acer stand and TP-Link extender remained undelivered at that point and said I intended to pursue a complaint. Flipkart reported delivering the extender the next day.

Gmail then reported a temporary delivery problem for a later message to the grievance address and said it would retry. That does not establish permanent non-delivery. It does mean that even the complaint about delays acquired a delay. By then, I was fluent in the language of things intending to arrive.

The box had a different number.

The same confirmation mentions 13,658 SuperCoins to be credited after the return period. The emails reviewed do not establish whether those coins were credited, their cash value, or the outcome of the pricing complaint.

The box, being rather old-fashioned, expressed its price in rupees. I sent support a photograph of the label so that the two numbers could be introduced. They had evidently been living separate lives.

Fair questions.

Because a delivery notice is not the same thing as the whole story.

Were the orders ever delivered?

Flipkart reported delivery of the mouse on 21 October, the Microsoft order on 25 October, and the TP-Link extender on 29 October 2026. The later outcome of the Acer stand is not established in the messages reviewed.

Was the mouse price higher than the box MRP?

The order confirmation’s ₹12,567 cash figure exceeds the photographed ₹9,573 MRP by ₹2,994, before rewards. I recall ₹12,570; the confirmation separately shows a three-coin indicator and mentions 13,658 SuperCoins to be credited later. Their eventual credit and cash value are not established here.

Were complaints closed without a fix?

No clear closure notice surfaced in the reviewed Gmail correspondence. In-app case histories were not available to this review, so this site does not claim to know their status.
