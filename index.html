04 / THE CASEBOOK

Five orders.
Several alternate realities.

Recorded dates. Actual replies. Commentary supplied by a customer who had time to spare. All statuses below are as reviewed on 6 November 2026.

01

The mouse

Logitech MX Vertical

Delivered · 6 days late

Promised 15 October. Delivered 21 October. Tracking offered this explanation:

“Delivery rescheduled as per customer request.”

I had requested the mouse. Apparently this was insufficiently specific. I had neglected to request that they give it to me.

Support also warned that the delivery caller’s number might appear as spam and end in 777. A purchase now required me to distinguish the person bringing my mouse from the person seeking my banking OTP.

By 6 November the mouse was home, but its support issue remained open. The product had escaped. Its paperwork had not.

02

The licences

Microsoft 365 Personal × 5

30 days from order to delivery

Ordered 25 September. Delivery reported 25 October. Thirty days for software keys: download speed measured in working weeks.

“Due to logistics issues, it couldn't be delivered today.”

I wondered whether the letters and numbers had stopped for chai at a toll plaza.

From 14 October, notices said the shipment “may have been lost or damaged while on its way to you”. By 17 October, three near-identical notices had arrived without a replacement being dispatched. The explanation was enjoying considerably better distribution than the product.

The order page eventually showed five delivered entries and four cancelled entries for five licences; the replacement request was marked complete. Nine outcomes for five items. At least something had exceeded expectations.

03

The extender

TP-Link Wi-Fi range extender

Delivered · 7 days late

Due 22 October. Delivered 29 October. At 1:19 a.m. on 23 October, an email informed me that yesterday’s delivery was running late. I had suspected as much, having been there.

At 1:14 a.m. on 30 October, after delivery, the chat reply said:

“I've checked your TP-Link RE200 WiFi Router, and it is scheduled for delivery tomorrow, 30 Oct, Friday.”

The extender had arrived. Support was still extending its arrival date.

04

The stand

Acer ZC.A01SI.179 laptop stand

Cancelled · replacement pending

Promised 21 October, moved to 22 October, then moved again. Support called this:

“systemic repromise being done.”

Yesterday’s promise had been promoted to tomorrow. No performance review appeared necessary.

Earlier assurances included: “Please don't worry, your order is safe and there is no indication of cancellation.” On 4 November at 9:41 p.m., an email said the stand “may have been lost or damaged while on its way to you”. At 10:13 p.m., another said it “could not be successfully delivered as it did not meet the product delivery guidelines.”

I had assumed the entrance requirement for delivery was turning up.

The original order was cancelled on 4 November; a replacement was expected on 11 November. On 6 November, with no stand delivered, the complaint was marked “resolved”. One of us was clearly using the word recreationally.

05

The trimmer

Philips MG3760/33 multigroomer

No delivery confirmed

Ordered 28 October. Expected 4 November. Still “out for delivery” on 6 November: a status so open-minded it refuses to acknowledge a calendar.

In chat, an agent said a priority ticket had been raised and an update would arrive on WhatsApp by 6 p.m. I asked whether this would simply repeat the next day. After another assurance, the chat produced:

“Please ignore above line.”

At last, an instruction consistent with the service. The captured records contain no delivery confirmation for the trimmer.

05 / THE CORRESPONDENCE

Your concern has
acquired colleagues.

The support and grievance trail, October–November 2026. Quoted replies are from the records. The department descriptions are mine.

17 October 2026

A modest request for the things I bought

I emailed the grievance mailbox under the subject “3 orders undelivered, wrong dates blamed on me - please fix”. I asked for delivery, corrected tracking, and one accountable human. No acknowledgement appears in the mail captured through 6 November.

The system had already decided that I requested the delays. It was remarkably efficient at obtaining my consent without the administrative burden of asking.

20 October 2026

The job titles got bigger

I escalated to Kalyan Krishnamurthy at Flipkart and John Furner at Walmart, copying the grievance mailbox and Walmart directors’ address. I attached order details and asked for an owner.

Customer Support replied about the mouse, requesting time until 21 October, 4:23 p.m. No named leadership recipient replied in the captured mail. The recipient list had reached the executive floor; the answer appeared to have used the service lift.

20 October 2026

BLACK membership. Queue included.

I had bought a 12-month Flipkart BLACK membership advertising:

“Priority Customer Support 24x7 no queue no bot”

The priority channel put me behind dozens of other customers. It was VIP darshan, except the deity kept asking me to check WhatsApp.

20 October 2026

The strawberry examination

I asked the representative to count the r’s in “strawberrry”—my deliberately altered spelling, with four in total. The reply answered a different word:

“There are 3 r's in strawberry! I'm a real agent here to help you.”

It also promised the Acer stand by 26 October. When challenged, the representative repeated the humanity assurance and delivery promise. This does not establish whether a bot was involved. It establishes that I paid for priority support and ended up supervising a fruit-based spelling exercise while my stand remained elsewhere.

23 October 2026

A specialist in yesterday

A polite phone representative could discuss the mouse, delivered two days earlier. My undelivered orders belonged to other teams. After being told those teams would contact me, I emailed the CEOs again; no written reply followed in the captured correspondence.

I had reached a human whose jurisdiction ended precisely where my problem began.

26 October 2026

Please provide proof of the proof

A promised specialist call within 24 hours did not happen. A grievance reply then stated:

“We see you havent contacted our support team about your concern.”

Below it sat fourteen earlier support messages. Apparently those had been a pen-pal scheme. I imagined taking the whole thread to an inquiry counter and being asked for a photocopy of the internet.

28–29 October 2026

The complaint also encountered logistics

On 28 October I sent the CEOs and grievance mailbox an email headed “legal notice”. It asked for a named owner, dispatch dates, the order-event audit trail, answers on BLACK membership, and a reply by 5 p.m. on 30 October. I cited the grievance rules and stated my intention to approach the Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission. This records what I sent, not a court finding.

The extender was delivered the next day; the records do not establish why. A later grievance follow-up received this Gmail notice:

“Temporary System Problem... Delivery delayed... will continue trying for 46 hours.”

My complaint about delivery delays had developed a delivery delay. The system was achieving thematic consistency.

2 November 2026

I supplied the closing reply

With no response from a named escalation recipient, I wrote:

“I give up, it seems no one at Flipkart or Walmart gives a damn about FK Customers. Thanks for the cautionary tale. Bess your heart.”

The typo is mine. At this stage even the blessing had suffered a fulfilment issue.

1–4 November 2026

Three deadlines. Please collect them all.

“We have escalated your issue to the concerned team.”

Three incident emails promised resolution on 7 November: at 11:55 a.m., 6:37 p.m., and 9:00 p.m. Three deadlines for one day. A raffle with apologies.

I picture the concerned team as a department whose principal responsibility is remaining concerned. It has excellent retention.

6 November 2026

The file arrived safely

The missing Acer stand’s delivery complaint was “resolved”; its replacement remained expected on 11 November. The delivered mouse’s issue was still “Open | Resolution by Tomorrow, Nov 07”. The TP-Link address-change case was also marked resolved. The captures do not identify who closed these cases or why.

No grievance acknowledgement or reply from a named leadership recipient appeared in the captured mail through this review. Customer Support had sent updates and made the mouse call.

06 / THE VERDICT

I have also
reached a resolution.

No concerned team was required.

I wanted five purchases. I received three deliveries, a collection of revised dates, and an unpaid internship in following up.

Delays happen. What made this worth documenting was the machinery around them: tracking that attributed changes to me, a grievance reply overlooking the support thread beneath it, and a missing stand whose complaint achieved closure ahead of the parcel.

This is one customer’s experience, recorded through 6 November 2026. My future orders will be placed elsewhere. Unlike the laptop stand, that decision has arrived.

THE PRACTICAL PART

Actual answers.
An exciting format.

Facts first. No ticket required.

Did the Flipkart orders arrive?

As reviewed on 6 November 2026, the mouse arrived on 21 October and the extender on 29 October. Notices reported the Microsoft licences delivered on 25 October. The original Acer stand order was cancelled on 4 November, with a replacement expected on 11 November. No delivery of the stand or trimmer was confirmed in the captured records.

Were the Microsoft licences 30 days late?

Thirty days elapsed between the 25 September order and delivery reported on 25 October. This account does not establish the original promised date, so that figure is order-to-delivery time, not a claim of 30 days overdue.

Did Flipkart or Walmart respond to the complaints?

Customer Support sent incident updates and a representative called about the mouse. Through 6 November, the captured mail contained no acknowledgement of the 17 October grievance and no reply from a named leadership recipient. That is narrower than saying nobody responded.

Does the strawberry exchange prove the agent was a bot?

No. It shows an answer about a different spelling and repeated assurances. Neither establishes who or what produced the replies. The joke is about the experience, not a verified diagnosis of the support system.

Is Avoid Flipkart an official support website?

No. This is an independent customer account with satirical commentary, not a Flipkart or Walmart service. It cannot look up orders, issue refunds or resolve complaints. Please do not share order numbers, payment details or OTPs here.
