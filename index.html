An independent account · reviewed 2 November 2026

I paid Flipkart for a mouse, five Microsoft licences and a Wi-Fi extender. I expected products. Flipkart offered me an immersive course in repeatedly explaining that I wanted products.

I learned that a delivery date can move without a parcel, that a shipment can be described as possibly lost or damaged in three separate notices, and that a grievance team can tell you that you never contacted support. In an email. About your support complaint.

Flipkart eventually reported delivering the three orders. Along the way came a paid “no bot” support promise, questions about a price above the box MRP, and a specialist call that, as I later wrote, never arrived. The emails proved much easier to deliver than the mouse.

The board above shows dates in 2026. For Microsoft 365, the first date is when the order was placed; its original delivery date was 30 September. The other dates are described in the account below.

The airport scene and product images are illustrations, not photographs of the actual deliveries.

The orders took the scenic route.

Three deliveries, as reported in the emails.

The mouse

Logitech MX Vertical

Promised 15 October, then revised to 16 October. Flipkart reported delivery on 21 October.

Six days late. I also received a notification saying I had requested a date change. I had asked for the mouse; I had not asked to rearrange the calendar around it.

The licences

Microsoft 365 Personal × 5

Ordered 25 September, due 30 September, then shifted to 1 October. On 14 October, Flipkart said the shipment might have been lost or damaged and a replacement would be sent. Delivery was reported on 25 October.

In my grievance, I counted three near-identical lost-or-damaged notices. The notices were being delivered with a reliability the replacement could only admire.

The extender

TP-Link Wi-Fi range extender

Due 22 October, then moved to 25 October. I wrote that the delay notice arrived at 1:19 a.m. on 23 October, after the first date had passed. Flipkart reported delivery on 29 October.

That is not advance notice. That is the calendar emailing its condolences.

A separate Acer laptop stand had not shipped when I wrote on 28 October. Its later outcome is not established in the emails reviewed.

Then came the emails.

Selected moments from the correspondence. The commentary is mine.

14 October 2026

Three emails, one missing shipment

Flipkart wrote that the Microsoft shipment “may have been lost or damaged while on its way to you” and that the seller would ship a new item. In my 17 October grievance, I said I had received three similar notices and no replacement had shipped by then.

Three versions of the same explanation are useful if you are collecting email. They are less useful if you are trying to collect Microsoft 365. I began to suspect the replacement notice had its own delivery team.

17 October 2026

Apparently, I rescheduled myself

I wrote about the delayed orders and disputed notifications saying I had requested delivery-date changes for the mouse and the Acer stand. I said I had made no such request and asked for the records to be corrected.

I had been asking for the items to move. Flipkart’s notifications suggested I had also been moving the dates. If my orders can make requests on my behalf, perhaps they can have a word with customer support. I have been trying.

20 October 2026

Dear leadership, where are my things?

I sent an escalation addressed to Kalyan Krishnamurthy and John Furner at Flipkart and Walmart addresses, asking about delivery records and priority support. The correspondence reviewed does not establish whether either executive personally read it.

My email now had impressive recipients. I still had no single person to contact about all the missing orders. The job titles had moved up. The responsibility was apparently still waiting for dispatch.

20 October 2026

Premium support and the strawberry examination

I had bought a 12-month Flipkart BLACK membership on 18 October. In my escalation I quoted its listing: “Priority Customer Support 24x7 no queue no bot.” I reported being directed to automated chats and questioned whether other repetitive replies came from people.

I asked how many times the letter “r” appears in “strawberry” (three) and received incorrect answers. A human can miscount letters too; this does not prove anyone was a bot. It does raise a question about the premium experience: why was I conducting a small census of possible robots instead of getting help with my orders?

23 October 2026

One customer, several departments

I described a call in which the representative could see the mouse order but said other teams would handle the others. The mouse had arrived by then; I was still asking for one accountable contact for the outstanding orders.

I had finally reached someone responsible for the one order that no longer needed following up. If customer support were a rescue operation, the boat would arrive to collect the people already standing on shore.

26 October 2026

The support email that denied support emails

“Your concern is now our top priority.”

An automated message said a specialist would get in touch within 24 hours. It came from an address that could not accept replies. I later wrote that the call had not arrived.

A separate grievance reply said, “We see you havent contacted our support team about your concern.” I already had earlier support emails in my inbox. Apparently, the people who sent those messages had been writing to me socially. It was kind of them to include updates about my missing orders.

I could forward the old emails as proof that the old emails exist. That would create another email whose existence might need to be established. At this rate, my inbox would require a grievance officer of its own.

28–29 October 2026

The escalation develops a delivery problem

I wrote that the Acer stand and TP-Link extender remained undelivered at that point and said I intended to pursue a complaint. Flipkart reported delivering the extender the next day.

Gmail then reported a temporary delivery problem for a later message to the grievance address and said it would retry. That does not establish permanent non-delivery. It does mean that a complaint about delivery delays acquired a delivery delay. I was tempted to escalate the escalation, but the grievance team might first need proof that I had contacted it.

The box had a different number.

The Logitech MX Vertical pricing question

Order confirmation₹12,567cash figure shown, plus a separate three-coin indicator
Photographed box MRP₹9,573including taxes, as printed on the label

The cash figure in the confirmation is ₹2,994 above the photographed MRP, before rewards. I recall a total charge of ₹12,570, which would make the difference ₹2,997; the email’s three-coin indicator is shown separately, and its cash value is not established here.

The same confirmation mentions 13,658 SuperCoins to be credited after the return period. The emails reviewed do not establish whether those coins were credited, their cash value, or the outcome of the pricing complaint.

The box and the order confirmation both speak rupees. The reward speaks SuperCoins, in the future tense.

I sent support a photograph of the label. ₹12,567 minus ₹9,573 is ₹2,994. I can do that calculation myself; what I cannot establish from the emails is whether anyone resolved the complaint. A subtraction and a reply would be an excellent start.
